====================================================================
        AI Quality Judgment Process Explanation Video - Narration Script
====================================================================
        Total Length: Approximately 10 minutes (time stamps per scene)
====================================================================


[Scene 1: Opening] (0:00 ~ 0:40)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Hello.

Today, I will explain how AI is utilized
for quality judgment in the battery recycling process.

(pause)

Let me start with a question.

"Can AI really learn everything on its own
and automatically produce optimal quality?"

To give you the conclusion first,
AI has clear boundaries and limitations in its role.

Through this video today,
I will clearly distinguish between
what humans must do and what AI can do.


[Scene 2: Process Overview - From Waste Batteries to Black Mass] (0:40 ~ 1:40)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

First, let's briefly look at the entire process.

The battery recycling process consists of three main stages.

First, primary crushing.
Using a twin-shaft shear crusher,
waste batteries are broken into pieces of 50 to 100 millimeters.

Second, secondary crushing.
Through a hammer crusher,
pieces are further reduced to 5 to 20 millimeters.

Third, the separation process.
Using an impact mill,
valuable metals are separated from the piece surfaces.

After this process, we finally obtain
"black mass," a powder containing valuable metals
such as lithium, cobalt, and nickel.

The two key goals of the process are:
high recovery rate and
uniform quality black mass production.


[Scene 3: The Core of Quality - Recovery Rate and Purity] (1:40 ~ 2:40)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

So how do we determine quality?

The most important indicator is the "recovery rate."
It represents the ratio of valuable metals actually recovered
compared to those contained in the input waste batteries.

"Component purity" is also important.
It indicates how much lithium, cobalt, and nickel
are contained in the black mass.

(pause)

Now, here's an important point.

This type of chemical component analysis
can only be accurately measured
through an ICP analyzer,
that is, Inductively Coupled Plasma Optical Emission Spectrometry.

Collecting black mass samples,
analyzing them with ICP equipment,
and verifying the results

(with emphasis)
must be performed by humans.

AI cannot measure chemical components in real-time.
Let me make this point clear first.


[Scene 4: Finding Optimal Conditions - The Human Role] (2:40 ~ 4:20)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Now for the key question.

"What equipment settings and environmental conditions
should we use to achieve the highest recovery rate?"

Let me explain the process of finding these optimal conditions.

(slowly, step by step)

Step 1.
Input waste battery cells into the process.

Step 2.
Set equipment and environmental conditions.

Equipment conditions include
blade specifications, rotation speed, and process flow rate.

Environmental conditions include
temperature, humidity, and pressure.

Step 3.
Produce black mass with the configured settings.

Step 4.
Collect samples from the produced black mass,
perform component analysis with ICP equipment,
and calculate the recovery rate.

(pause)

If the recovery rate doesn't reach the target,
change the equipment or environmental conditions
and repeat from Step 1.

This process may require dozens of repeated experiments.

(emphasis)
And the important point is,
AI does not intervene in any of this process.

Finding the optimal recovery rate conditions
is entirely accomplished through human experimentation and judgment.


[Scene 5: The Question - Where is AI Used?] (4:20 ~ 5:00)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Naturally, you might wonder at this point.

"Then where exactly is AI used?"

"If AI cannot analyze chemical components in real-time,
what can AI actually learn?"

(pause)

Good question.
This is exactly the core of today's explanation.


[Scene 6: What AI Learns - Physical Size Optimization] (5:00 ~ 6:30)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

What AI learns is

(emphasis)
"the physical size of crushed materials."

Specifically,
it measures and manages the particle size distribution
of black mass powder.

Particle size distribution can be measured
in real-time using laser diffraction sensors.

The measured values include D10, D50, and D90,
which represent the particle sizes at
the lower 10 percent, median, and upper 90 percent
of the particle size distribution.

(pause)

Now, here's the key connection.

Earlier, I mentioned that humans find
optimal recovery rate conditions through repeated experiments.

Something is discovered during that experimentation process.

"At a specific particle size distribution, recovery rate is highest."

For example,
when D50 is 15 micrometers
and the distribution deviation is within plus or minus 2 micrometers,
the recovery rate was highest at 94 percent.

Humans discover this correlation first.

(slowly)
Then,
when we set that particle size distribution as the "target" for AI,
AI learns the optimal equipment and environmental conditions
to maintain that particle size distribution.

In other words,
AI doesn't learn the chemical recovery rate itself,
but learns the physical size that correlates with recovery rate.


[Scene 7: AI Learning Process - Step by Step Explanation] (6:30 ~ 7:50)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Let me organize the AI learning process in three stages.

(first stage)
Stage 1, Goal Setting.
This is decided by humans.

Through multiple repeated experiments, we confirm optimal recovery rate conditions,
record the particle size distribution characteristics under those conditions,
and tell the AI "this particle size distribution is the target."

For example,
D10 is 5 micrometers,
D50 is 15 micrometers,
D90 is 30 micrometers,
standard deviation within 8 micrometers.

We define targets like this.

(second stage)
Stage 2, Data Collection.

Input data includes equipment and environmental conditions.
Blade angle, rotation speed, feed rate, temperature, humidity, pressure, and so on.

Output data is the measured particle size distribution.
D10, D50, D90 values and standard deviation.

While operating the process,
we accumulate combination data of inputs and outputs.

(third stage)
Stage 3, Model Training.

AI receives input data
and trains a model to predict particle size distribution.

It compares predicted values with actual measurements
and improves the model to reduce errors.

When training is complete,
AI understands the relationship:
"with these equipment and environmental conditions,
this particle size distribution will result."


[Scene 8: AI Application - Role in Mass Production] (7:50 ~ 8:50)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

So, what role does AI play
in the mass production stage?

(explanation)
AI monitors particle size distribution in real-time.
24 hours a day, without rest.

When particle size data comes from sensors,
AI compares it against the target range.

If the D50 value exceeds 18 micrometers,
AI issues an adjustment command to increase rotation speed.

Conversely, if D50 is below 12 micrometers,
it commands to decrease rotation speed.

If distribution deviation suddenly increases,
it sends a warning alert to inspect blade condition.

This is how AI performs real-time feedback control.

(emphasis)
Here's the key point.

AI only manages physical size.
Chemical recovery rate is still managed by humans
through periodic ICP sampling.

However, if particle size is maintained consistently,
recovery rate also remains stable,
as this correlation has already been verified through human experimentation.

AI's particle size management therefore
directly contributes to quality stabilization.


[Scene 9: Summary - Division of Roles Between Humans and AI] (8:50 ~ 9:40)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Let me summarize what we've covered so far.

(human role)
What humans do is as follows.

First, design initial experiments.
Decide what conditions to try.

Second, perform ICP component analysis.
Chemical analysis can only be done by humans.

Third, calculate and evaluate recovery rates.
Judge whether targets are achieved.

Fourth, determine optimal conditions.
Final decision-making belongs to humans.

Fifth, set target particle size for AI.
Tell the AI what particle size indicates good quality.

(AI role)
What AI does is as follows.

First, real-time particle size monitoring.
Monitoring 24 hours without interruption.

Second, alert when deviations occur.
Immediately notify of problems.

Third, automatically adjust equipment conditions.
Perform feedback control.

Fourth, learn particle size patterns.
Understand relationships between equipment and particle size.

Fifth, detect anomalies early.
Prevent equipment failures.

(collaboration result)
In the end, humans define "what good quality is,"
and AI automatically manages "maintaining that quality."

The result of this collaboration is
consistent quality, high productivity, and low defect rates.


[Scene 10: Closing - Implementation Benefits and Conclusion] (9:40 ~ 10:00)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Let me share the benefits of implementing the AI quality management system.

Particle size deviation decreased by 40 percent,
and quality defect rate decreased by 60 percent.
Manual inspection time was reduced by 70 percent,
and equipment utilization improved by 15 percent.

(conclusion, slowly)

AI is not magic.
It can only learn and manage what is measurable.

But within that scope,
AI is faster, more accurate, and tireless compared to humans.

When human expertise meets AI's real-time management capability,
we can achieve the highest quality and efficiency.

Thank you for watching.
If you have any questions, please visit our booth for consultation.
