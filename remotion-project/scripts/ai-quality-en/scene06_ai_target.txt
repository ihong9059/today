What AI learns is the physical size of crushed materials.

Specifically, it measures and manages the particle size distribution of black mass powder.

Particle size distribution can be measured in real-time using laser diffraction sensors.

The measured values include D10, D50, and D90, which represent the particle sizes at the lower 10 percent, median, and upper 90 percent of the particle size distribution.

Now, here's the key connection.

Earlier, I mentioned that humans find optimal recovery rate conditions through repeated experiments.

Something is discovered during that experimentation process. At a specific particle size distribution, recovery rate is highest.

For example, when D50 is 15 micrometers and the distribution deviation is within plus or minus 2 micrometers, the recovery rate was highest at 94 percent.

Humans discover this correlation first.

Then, when we set that particle size distribution as the target for AI, AI learns the optimal equipment and environmental conditions to maintain that particle size distribution.

In other words, AI doesn't learn the chemical recovery rate itself, but learns the physical size that correlates with recovery rate.
